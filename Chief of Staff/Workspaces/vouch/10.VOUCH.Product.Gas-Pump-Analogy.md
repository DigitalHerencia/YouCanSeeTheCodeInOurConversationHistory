# Gas Pump Analogy

A Stripe PaymentIntent is basically the lifecycle object for a payment attempt. Think of it as the authoritative record of “this money movement” from creation through final outcome.

## States

For Vouch, the important states are:

- You create a PaymentIntent for the full amount the customer will pay. That total includes the Vouch amount plus fees according to your pricing model.

- Then Stripe attempts payment authorization. Authorization is like the gas station hold. The bank says, “these funds are reserved for capture.” The customer’s available balance drops, but settlement is not final yet. Money has not fully moved in the accounting sense.

That maps nicely to Vouch because your core mechanic is: reserve economic consequence now, decide final outcome later.

## Outcomes

Then one of a few things happens.

- If both parties confirm within the window, Vouch tells Stripe to capture the authorized funds. Stripe settles the payment, takes its fee, your application fee is carved out, and the remainder routes according to your Connect configuration to the connected account. That is final settlement.

- If conditions fail before capture, Vouch cancels the PaymentIntent or lets the authorization expire, depending on timing and state. The hold drops off and funds return to availability for the payer. Similar to the gas station releasing the unused hold.

- If the money was already captured for some reason and the rules later require reversal, then Vouch issues a refund. That is different from releasing a hold. A refund is money moving back after settlement.

## Hold v. Refund

That distinction matters

- Authorization is a hold.
- Capture is settlement.
- Cancel is releasing the hold.
- Refund is reversing settled money.

For Vouch, you generally want to live in authorization land until outcome is known, then either capture or cancel. That is cleaner than capturing early and refunding later whenever possible.

## Connect

Your platform account on Stripe is the orchestrator. The connected account is the merchant/provider receiving proceeds.

With application fees, Stripe lets the platform collect a fee while routing the remaining funds appropriately. In plain English:

1. Customer pays total.
2. Stripe processes payment.
3. Stripe fee comes out.
4. Vouch platform fee is collected.
5. Merchant receives remainder.

That matches your model.

## Onboarding.

Stripe Connect onboarding is just Stripe verifying the connected account can legally receive payouts: identity, business details, bank account, tax/KYC, capability activation. That is your payout readiness gate.

## Webhooks.

Webhooks are Stripe calling your server and saying:

- Payment authorized.
- Payment failed.
- Payment captured.
- Refund completed.
- Account capability updated.
- Payout account restricted.
- Charge disputed, and so on.

That is how your local database stays aligned with provider truth.

## The Process

For Vouch specifically, the clean mapping is:

1. Customer payment begins by creating a PaymentIntent.
2. PaymentIntent authorized means Vouch state becomes funded or reserved.
3. Invite accepted means workflow advances, but money state does not change.
4. Dual confirmation means capture PaymentIntent.
5. Failed confirmation means cancel authorization or refund if already captured.
6. Stripe webhook confirms actual provider outcome.
7. Local state reconciles.
8. Audit event written.

***Done.***
