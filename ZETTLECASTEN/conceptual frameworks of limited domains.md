In [information science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_science "Information science"), an **ontology** encompasses a representation, formal naming, and definitions of the categories, properties, and relations between the concepts, data, or entities that pertain to one, many, or all [domains of discourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_of_discourse "Domain of discourse"). More simply, an ontology is a way of showing the properties of a subject area and how they are related, by defining a set of terms and relational expressions that represent the entities in that subject area. The field which studies ontologies so conceived is sometimes referred to as *applied ontology*.[^1]

Every [academic discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_discipline "Academic discipline") or field, in creating its terminology, thereby lays the groundwork for an ontology. Each uses ontological assumptions to frame explicit theories, research and applications. Improved ontologies may improve problem solving within that domain, [interoperability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interoperability "Interoperability") of data systems, and discoverability of data. Translating research papers within every field is a problem made easier when experts from different countries maintain a [controlled vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_vocabulary "Controlled vocabulary") of [jargon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jargon "Jargon") between each of their languages.[^2] For instance, the [definition and ontology of economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_and_economics#Definition_and_ontology_of_economics "Philosophy and economics") is a primary concern in [Marxist economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist_economics "Marxist economics"),[^3] but also in other [subfields of economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_economics#Branches_of_economics "Outline of economics").[^4] An example of economics relying on information science occurs in cases where a simulation or model is intended to enable economic decisions, such as determining what [capital assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_asset "Capital asset") are at risk and by how much (see [risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management "Risk management")).

What ontologies in both [information science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_science "Information science") and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy") have in common is the attempt to represent entities, including both objects and events, with all their interdependent properties and relations, according to a system of categories. In both fields, there is considerable work on problems of [ontology engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_engineering "Ontology engineering") (e.g., [Quine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Van_Orman_Quine "Willard Van Orman Quine") and [Kripke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Kripke "Saul Kripke") in philosophy, [Sowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Sowa "John F. Sowa") and [Guarino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Guarino "Nicola Guarino") in information science),[^5] and debates concerning to what extent [normative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normative "Normative") ontology is possible (e.g., [foundationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundationalism "Foundationalism") and [coherentism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherentism "Coherentism") in philosophy, [BFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_formal_ontology "Basic formal ontology") and [Cyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyc "Cyc") in artificial intelligence).

[Applied ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_ontology "Applied ontology") is considered by some as a successor to prior work in philosophy. However many current efforts are more concerned with establishing [controlled vocabularies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_vocabularies "Controlled vocabularies") of narrow domains than with philosophical [first principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_principles "First principles"), or with questions such as the mode of existence of [fixed essences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essence "Essence") or whether enduring objects (e.g., [perdurantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perdurantism "Perdurantism") and [endurantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endurantism "Endurantism")) may be ontologically more primary than [processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Process "Process"). [Artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") has retained considerable attention regarding [applied ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_ontology "Applied ontology") in subfields like [natural language processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing "Natural language processing") within [machine translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_translation "Machine translation") and [knowledge representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_representation "Knowledge representation"), but ontology editors are being used often in a range of fields, including biomedical informatics [^6] and industry.[^7] Such efforts often use ontology editing tools such as [Protégé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prot%C3%A9g%C3%A9_\(software\) "Protégé (software)").[^8]

## Ontology in philosophy

[Ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology "Ontology") is a branch of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy "Philosophy") and intersects areas such as [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics "Metaphysics"), [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology "Epistemology"), and [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language "Philosophy of language"), as it considers how knowledge, language, and perception relate to the nature of reality. [Metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics "Metaphysics") deals with questions like "what exists?" and "what is the nature of reality?". One of five traditional branches of philosophy, metaphysics is concerned with exploring existence through properties, entities and relations such as those between [particulars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular "Particular") and [universals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_\(metaphysics\) "Universal (metaphysics)"), [intrinsic and extrinsic properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrinsic_and_extrinsic_properties_\(philosophy\) "Intrinsic and extrinsic properties (philosophy)"), or [essence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essence "Essence") and [existence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existence "Existence"). Metaphysics has been an ongoing topic of discussion since recorded history.

## Etymology

The [compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_\(linguistics\) "Compound (linguistics)") word *ontology* combines *[onto](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/onto- "wikt:onto-") -*, from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") [ὄν](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BD%A4%CE%BD "wikt:ὤν"), *on* ([gen.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genitive "Genitive") ὄντος, *ontos*), i.e. "being; that which is", which is the [present](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Present_tense "Present tense") [participle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participle "Participle") of the [verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verb "Verb") [εἰμί](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%B5%E1%BC%B0%CE%BC%CE%AF "wikt:εἰμί"), *eimí*, i.e. "to be, I am", and [\-λογία](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/-%CE%BB%CE%BF%CE%B3%CE%AF%CE%B1 "wikt:-λογία"), *[\-logia](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/-logia "wikt:-logia")*, i.e. "logical discourse", see [classical compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_compound "Classical compound") for this type of word formation.[^9] [^10]

While the [etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology "Etymology") is Greek, the oldest extant record of the word itself, the [Neo-Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Latin "Neo-Latin") form *ontologia*, appeared in 1606 in the work *[Ogdoas Scholastica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogdoas_Scholastica "Ogdoas Scholastica")* by [Jacob Lorhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Lorhard "Jacob Lorhard") (*Lorhardus*) and in 1613 in the *[Lexicon philosophicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicon_philosophicum?action=edit&redlink=1 "Lexicon philosophicum (page does not exist)")* by [Rudolf Göckel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_G%C3%B6ckel "Rudolf Göckel") (*Goclenius*).[^11]

The first occurrence in English of *ontology* as recorded by the *OED* (*[Oxford English Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary "Oxford English Dictionary")*, online edition, 2008) came in *[Archeologia Philosophica Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archeologia_Philosophica_Nova?action=edit&redlink=1 "Archeologia Philosophica Nova (page does not exist)")* or *New Principles of Philosophy* (1663) by [Gideon Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gideon_Harvey "Gideon Harvey").

## Formal ontology

Since the mid-1970s, researchers in the field of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") (AI) have recognized that [knowledge engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_engineering "Knowledge engineering") is the key to building large and powerful AI systems. AI researchers argued that they could create new ontologies as [computational models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_model "Computational model") that enable certain kinds of [automated reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_reasoning "Automated reasoning"), which was only [marginally successful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_winter "AI winter"). In the 1980s, the AI community began to use the term *ontology* to refer to both a theory of a modeled world and a component of [knowledge-based systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge-based_systems "Knowledge-based systems"). In particular, David Powers introduced the word *ontology* to AI to refer to real world or robotic grounding,[^12] [^13] publishing in 1990 literature reviews emphasizing grounded ontology in association with the call for papers for a AAAI Summer Symposium Machine Learning of Natural Language and Ontology, with an expanded version published in SIGART Bulletin and included as a preface to the proceedings.[^14] Some researchers, drawing inspiration from philosophical ontologies, viewed computational ontology as a kind of applied philosophy.[^15]

In 1993, the widely cited web page and paper "Toward Principles for the Design of Ontologies Used for Knowledge Sharing" by [Tom Gruber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Gruber "Tom Gruber") [^16] used *ontology* as a technical term in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science") closely related to earlier idea of [semantic networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_networks "Semantic networks") and [taxonomies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_\(general\) "Taxonomy (general)"). Gruber introduced the term as *a specification of a conceptualization*:

> An ontology is a description (like a formal specification of a program) of the concepts and relationships that can formally exist for an agent or a community of agents. This definition is consistent with the usage of ontology as set of concept definitions, but more general. And it is a different sense of the word than its use in philosophy.[^17]

Attempting to distance ontologies from taxonomies and similar efforts in [knowledge modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_modeling "Knowledge modeling") that rely on [classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_\(computer_programming\) "Class (computer programming)") and [inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inheritance_\(object-oriented_programming\) "Inheritance (object-oriented programming)"), Gruber stated (1993):

> Ontologies are often equated with taxonomic hierarchies of classes, class definitions, and the subsumption relation, but ontologies need not be limited to these forms. Ontologies are also not limited to *conservative definitions*, that is, definitions in the traditional logic sense that only introduce terminology and do not add any knowledge about the world (Enderton, 1972). To specify a conceptualization, one needs to state axioms that *do* constrain the possible interpretations for the defined terms.[^16]

Recent experimental ontology frameworks have also explored resonance-based AI-human co-evolution structures, such as IAMF (Illumination AI Matrix Framework), OntoMotoOS (a meta-operating system concept for ethical and ontological AI–human co-evolution), and PSRT (Phase-Structural Reality Theory across multi-scale ontological layers). Though not yet widely adopted in academic discourse, such models propose phased approaches to ethical harmonization and structural emergence.[^18]

As refinement of Gruber's definition Feilmayr and Wöß (2016) stated: "An ontology is a formal, explicit specification of a shared conceptualization that is characterized by high semantic expressiveness required for increased complexity." [^19]

## Formal ontology components

Contemporary ontologies share many structural similarities, regardless of the language in which they are expressed. Most ontologies describe individuals (instances), classes (concepts), attributes and relations.

### Types

#### Domain ontology

A domain ontology (or domain-specific ontology) represents concepts which belong to a realm of the world, such as biology or politics. Each domain ontology typically models domain-specific definitions of terms. For example, the word *[card](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/card "wikt:card")* has many different meanings. An ontology about the domain of [poker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poker "Poker") would model the " [playing card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playing_card "Playing card") " meaning of the word, while an ontology about the domain of [computer hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_hardware "Computer hardware") would model the " [punched card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punched_card "Punched card") " and " [video card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_card "Video card") " meanings.

Since domain ontologies are written by different people, they represent concepts in very specific and unique ways, and are often incompatible within the same project. As systems that rely on domain ontologies expand, they often need to merge domain ontologies by hand-tuning each entity or using a combination of software merging and hand-tuning. This presents a challenge to the ontology designer. Different ontologies in the same domain arise due to different languages, different intended usage of the ontologies, and different perceptions of the domain (based on cultural background, education, ideology, etc.).

At present, merging ontologies that are not developed from a common [upper ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology "Upper ontology") is a largely manual process and therefore time-consuming and expensive. Domain ontologies that use the same upper ontology to provide a set of basic elements with which to specify the meanings of the domain ontology entities can be merged with less effort. There are studies on generalized techniques for merging ontologies,[^20] but this area of research is still ongoing, and it is a recent event to see the issue sidestepped by having multiple domain ontologies using the same upper ontology like the [OBO Foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OBO_Foundry "OBO Foundry").

### Upper ontology

An upper ontology (or foundation ontology) is a model of the commonly shared relations and objects that are generally applicable across a wide range of domain ontologies. It usually employs a [core glossary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_glossary "Core glossary") that overarches the terms and associated object descriptions as they are used in various relevant domain ontologies.

Standardized upper ontologies available for use include [BFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Formal_Ontology "Basic Formal Ontology"), [Dublin Core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Core "Dublin Core"), [GFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Formal_Ontology "General Formal Ontology"), [Cyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyc "Cyc"), [SUMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suggested_Upper_Merged_Ontology "Suggested Upper Merged Ontology"), [UMBEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMBEL "UMBEL"), and [DOLCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology#DOLCE "Upper ontology").[^21] [^22] [WordNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WordNet "WordNet") has been considered an upper ontology by some and has been used as a linguistic tool for learning domain ontologies.[^23]

### Hybrid ontology

The Gellish ontology is an example of a combination of an upper and a domain ontology.

## Visualization

A survey of ontology visualization methods is presented by Katifori et al.[^24] An updated survey of ontology visualization methods and tools was published by Dudás et al.[^25] The most established ontology visualization methods, namely indented tree and graph visualization are evaluated by Fu et al.[^26] A visual language for ontologies represented in [OWL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_Ontology_Language "Web Ontology Language") is specified by the *Visual Notation for OWL Ontologies (VOWL)*.[^27]

## Engineering

Ontology engineering (also called ontology building) is a set of tasks related to the development of ontologies for a particular domain.[^28] It is a subfield of [knowledge engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_engineering "Knowledge engineering") that studies the ontology development process, the ontology life cycle, the methods and methodologies for building ontologies, and the tools and languages that support them.[^29] [^30]

Ontology engineering aims to make explicit the knowledge contained in software applications, and organizational procedures for a particular domain. Ontology engineering offers a direction for overcoming semantic obstacles, such as those related to the definitions of business terms and software classes. Known challenges with ontology engineering include:

1. Ensuring the ontology is *current* with [domain knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_knowledge "Domain knowledge") and term use
2. Providing *sufficient specificity and concept coverage* for the domain of interest, thus minimizing the [content completeness problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_completeness_problem "Content completeness problem")
3. Ensuring the ontology can support its use cases

### Editors

**Ontology editors** are applications designed to assist in the creation or manipulation of ontologies. It is common for ontology editors to use one or more [ontology languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_language_\(computer_science\) "Ontology language (computer science)").

Aspects of ontology editors include: visual navigation possibilities within the [knowledge model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_model "Knowledge model"), [inference engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inference_engine "Inference engine") and [information extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_extraction "Information extraction"); support for modules; the import and export of foreign [knowledge representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_representation "Knowledge representation") languages for [ontology matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_matching "Ontology matching"); and the support of meta-ontologies such as [OWL-S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OWL-S "OWL-S"), [Dublin Core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Core "Dublin Core"), etc.[^31]

### Learning

Ontology learning is the automatic or semi-automatic creation of ontologies, including extracting a domain's terms from natural language text. As building ontologies manually is extremely labor-intensive and time-consuming, there is great motivation to automate the process. Information extraction and [text mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_mining "Text mining") have been explored to automatically link ontologies to documents, for example in the context of the BioCreative challenges.[^32]

### Research

Epistemological assumptions, which in research asks "What do you know? or "How do you know it?", creates the foundation researchers use when approaching a certain topic or area for potential research. As epistemology is directly linked to knowledge and how we come about accepting certain truths, individuals conducting academic research must understand what allows them to begin theory building. Simply, epistemological assumptions force researchers to question how they arrive at the knowledge they have.

## Languages

An [ontology language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_language "Ontology language") is a [formal language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_language "Formal language") used to encode an ontology. There are a number of such languages for ontologies, both proprietary and standards-based:

- [Common Algebraic Specification Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Algebraic_Specification_Language "Common Algebraic Specification Language") is a general logic-based specification language developed within the IFIP working group 1.3 "Foundations of System Specifications" and is a *de facto* standard language for software specifications. It is now being applied to ontology specifications in order to provide modularity and structuring mechanisms.
- [Common logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_logic "Common logic") is ISO standard 24707, a specification of a family of ontology languages that can be accurately translated into each other.
- The [Cyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyc "Cyc") project has its own ontology language called [CycL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CycL "CycL"), based on [first-order predicate calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_predicate_calculus "First-order predicate calculus") with some higher-order extensions.
- [DOGMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DOGMA "DOGMA") (Developing Ontology-Grounded Methods and Applications) adopts the fact-oriented modeling approach to provide a higher level of semantic stability.
- The Gellish language includes rules for its own extension and thus integrates an ontology with an ontology language.
- [IDEF5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDEF5 "IDEF5") is a [software engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineering "Software engineering") method to develop and maintain usable, accurate, domain ontologies.
- [KIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_Interchange_Format "Knowledge Interchange Format") is a syntax for [first-order logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_logic "First-order logic") that is based on [S-expressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-expression "S-expression"). SUO-KIF is a derivative version supporting the [Suggested Upper Merged Ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suggested_Upper_Merged_Ontology "Suggested Upper Merged Ontology").
- [MOF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-Object_Facility "Meta-Object Facility") and [UML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Modeling_Language "Unified Modeling Language") are standards of the [OMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_Management_Group "Object Management Group")
- [Olog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olog "Olog") is a [category theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_theory "Category theory") approach to ontologies, emphasizing translations between ontologies using [functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functor "Functor").
- [OBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Biomedical_Ontologies "Open Biomedical Ontologies"), a language used for biological and biomedical ontologies.
- [OntoUML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OntoUML "OntoUML") is an ontologically well-founded profile of UML for conceptual modeling of domain ontologies.
- [OWL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_Ontology_Language "Web Ontology Language") is a language for making ontological statements, developed as a follow-on from [RDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_Description_Framework "Resource Description Framework") and [RDFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RDFS "RDFS"), as well as earlier ontology language projects including [OIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_Inference_Layer "Ontology Inference Layer"), [DAML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA_Agent_Markup_Language "DARPA Agent Markup Language"), and [DAML+OIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAMLplusOIL "DAMLplusOIL"). OWL is intended to be used over the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web"), and all its elements (classes, properties and individuals) are defined as RDF [resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_resource "Web resource"), and identified by [URIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Resource_Identifier "Uniform Resource Identifier").
- [Rule Interchange Format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_Interchange_Format "Rule Interchange Format") (RIF) and [F-Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-Logic "F-Logic") combine ontologies and rules.
- [Semantic Application Design Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_Application_Design_Language "Semantic Application Design Language") (SADL) [^33] captures a subset of the expressiveness of [OWL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_Ontology_Language "Web Ontology Language"), using an English-like language entered via an [Eclipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_\(software\) "Eclipse (software)") Plug-in.
- [SBVR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SBVR "SBVR") (Semantics of Business Vocabularies and Rules) is an OMG standard adopted in industry to build ontologies.
- [TOVE Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOVE_Project "TOVE Project"), TOronto Virtual Enterprise project

## Published examples

- [Arabic Ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_Ontology "Arabic Ontology"), a linguistic ontology for Arabic, which can be used as an Arabic Wordnet but with ontologically-clean content.[^34]
- AURUM – Information Security Ontology,[^35] An ontology for [information security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_security "Information security") knowledge sharing, enabling users to collaboratively understand and extend the domain knowledge body. It may serve as a basis for automated information security risk and compliance management.
- [BabelNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BabelNet "BabelNet"), a very large multilingual semantic network and ontology, lexicalized in many languages
- [Basic Formal Ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Formal_Ontology "Basic Formal Ontology"),[^36] a formal upper ontology designed to support scientific research
- BioPAX,[^37] an ontology for the exchange and interoperability of biological pathway (cellular processes) data
- BMO,[^38] an e-Business Model Ontology based on a review of enterprise ontologies and business model literature
- SSBMO,[^39] a Strongly Sustainable Business Model Ontology based on a review of the systems based natural and social science literature (including business). Includes critique of and significant extensions to the Business Model Ontology (BMO).
- Common Core Ontologies (CCO),[^40] a suite of eleven ontologies that extend [Basic Formal Ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Formal_Ontology "Basic Formal Ontology"), which were developed under an [IARPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Advanced_Research_Projects_Activity "Intelligence Advanced Research Projects Activity") Knowledge Discovery and Dissemination grant and are widely-used in US defense and intelligence sectors.
- CCO and GexKB,[^41] Application Ontologies (APO) that integrate diverse types of knowledge with the Cell Cycle Ontology (CCO) and the Gene Expression Knowledge Base (GexKB)
- CContology (Customer Complaint Ontology),[^42] an e-business ontology to support online customer complaint management
- [CIDOC Conceptual Reference Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIDOC_Conceptual_Reference_Model "CIDOC Conceptual Reference Model"), an ontology for [cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage "Cultural heritage") [^43]
- COSMO,[^44] a Foundation Ontology (current version in OWL) that is designed to contain representations of all of the primitive concepts needed to logically specify the meanings of any domain entity. It is intended to serve as a basic ontology that can be used to translate among the representations in other ontologies or databases. It started as a merger of the basic elements of the OpenCyc and SUMO ontologies, and has been supplemented with other ontology elements (types, relations) so as to include representations of all of the words in the [Longman dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longman_Dictionary_of_Contemporary_English "Longman Dictionary of Contemporary English") [defining vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defining_vocabulary "Defining vocabulary").
- [Computer Science Ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Science_Ontology "Computer Science Ontology"), an automatically generated ontology of research topics in the field of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science")
- [Cyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyc "Cyc"), a large Foundation Ontology for formal representation of the universe of discourse
- [Disease Ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease_Ontology "Disease Ontology"),[^45] designed to facilitate the mapping of diseases and associated conditions to particular medical codes
- [DOLCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology#DOLCE "Upper ontology"), a Descriptive Ontology for Linguistic and Cognitive Engineering [^21] [^22]
- Drammar, ontology of drama [^46]
- [Dublin Core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Core "Dublin Core"), a simple ontology for documents and publishing
- Financial Industry Business Ontology (FIBO), a business conceptual ontology for the financial industry [^47]
- Foundational, Core and Linguistic Ontologies [^48]
- [Foundational Model of Anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundational_Model_of_Anatomy "Foundational Model of Anatomy"),[^49] an ontology for human anatomy
- [Friend of a Friend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FOAF_\(software\) "FOAF (software)"), an ontology for describing persons, their activities and their relations to other people and objects

## Libraries

The development of ontologies has led to the emergence of services providing lists or directories of ontologies called ontology libraries.

The following are libraries of human-selected ontologies.

- COLORE [^74] is an open repository of first-order ontologies in [Common Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Logic "Common Logic") with formal links between ontologies in the repository.
- DAML Ontology Library [^75] maintains a legacy of ontologies in DAML.
- Ontology Design Patterns portal [^76] is a wiki repository of reusable components and practices for ontology design, and also maintains a list of *exemplary ontologies*.
- Protégé Ontology Library [^77] contains a set of OWL, Frame-based and other format ontologies.
- SchemaWeb [^78] is a directory of RDF schemata expressed in RDFS, OWL and DAML+OIL.

The following are both directories and search engines.

- [OBO Foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OBO_Foundry "OBO Foundry") is a suite of interoperable reference ontologies in biology and biomedicine.[^59] [^79]
- Bioportal (ontology repository of NCBO) [^6]
- [Linked Open Vocabularies](https://lov.linkeddata.es/dataset/lov) [Archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20191106125054/https://lov.linkeddata.es/dataset/lov/) 2019-11-06 at the [Wayback Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayback_Machine "Wayback Machine")
- OntoSelect [^80] Ontology Library offers similar services for RDF/S, DAML and OWL ontologies.
- Ontaria [^81] is a "searchable and browsable directory of semantic web data" with a focus on RDF vocabularies with OWL ontologies. (NB Project "on hold" since 2004).
- [Swoogle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swoogle "Swoogle") is a directory and search engine for all RDF resources available on the Web, including ontologies.
- Open Ontology Repository initiative [^82]
- ROMULUS is a foundational ontology repository aimed at improving semantic interoperability. Currently there are three foundational ontologies in the repository: [DOLCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_ontology#DOLCE "Upper ontology"), [BFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Formal_Ontology "Basic Formal Ontology") and [GFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_formal_ontology "General formal ontology").[^83]

## Examples of applications

In general, ontologies can be used beneficially in several fields.

- Enterprise applications.[^84] A more concrete example is [SAPPHIRE (Health care)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAPPHIRE_\(Health_care\) "SAPPHIRE (Health care)") or *Situational Awareness and Preparedness for Public Health Incidences and Reasoning Engines* which is a [semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics "Semantics") -based [health information system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_information_system "Health information system") capable of tracking and evaluating situations and occurrences that may affect [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health "Public health").
- [Geographic information systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_information_systems "Geographic information systems") bring together data from different sources and benefit therefore from ontological metadata which helps to connect the semantics of the data.[^85]
- Domain-specific ontologies are extremely important in biomedical research, which requires named entity disambiguation of various biomedical terms and abbreviations that have the same string of characters but represent different biomedical concepts. For example, CSF can represent Colony Stimulating Factor or Cerebral Spinal Fluid, both of which are represented by the same term, CSF, in biomedical literature.[^86] This is why a large number of public ontologies are related to the life sciences. Life science data science tools that fail to implement these types of biomedical ontologies will not be able to accurately determine causal relationships between concepts.[^87]
