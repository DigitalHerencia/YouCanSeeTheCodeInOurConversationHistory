## But Let’s Be Honest—I Was Always a Social Engineer.

---

At the end of 2025, my ChatGPT Year-in-Review flashed across the screen like some soft-focus graduation slide show.

_“You explored 217 topics.”_  
_“You wrote over 1.3 million words.”_  
_“You asked 83 questions about how to fix CI/CD pipelines while clearly having an emotional breakdown.”_  
And then it hit me with the final punch:

> _“You're an engineer.”_

And I sat there—barely hydrated, emotionally half-compiled—and all I could think was:

> _“No, I’m not. I’m a social engineer with version control.”_

---

## The Lie We Tell The Stack

I don’t debug. I _negotiate_.  
I don’t code—I _manipulate abstractions until they comply_.  
You think I’m building systems?  
I’m building _trust_, baby. Between modules. Between coworkers. Between reality and the README.

Every “working build” I’ve ever shipped was actually:

- a political compromise between three competing libraries
    
- a charm offensive against the CI pipeline
    
- and a whispered lie to myself that “this is scalable”
    

---

## Dev Rel? Nah. Dev _Deception_

You think those pull requests were technical?  
They were _social theater_.  
A dance of carefully worded comments and emoji reactions.  
I merged that feature not because it worked—  
but because I made Tim from backend _believe_ it would.

My contribution wasn’t code.  
It was _vibes._

---

## The Terminal Is Just a Stage

You ever sit in a Zoom call and explain a bug you barely understand with enough confidence that someone else apologizes for it?

That’s not debugging.  
That’s _psychological load balancing._

I don’t run scripts.  
I run _simulations_ of competence until the problem goes away.

I’m not a developer.  
I’m a con artist with a GitHub streak.

---

## My NPM Package Is Just a Cry For Help

I once published a utility that didn’t do anything but `console.log("I'm trying my best.")`

Three people starred it.  
One guy forked it.  
Another made a pull request to add support for **emotional dark mode.**

You tell me who the engineer is now.

---

## Engineering Is Just Applied Storytelling

And the story I’ve been telling?  
That I know what I’m doing.  
That I’m not guessing every single day.  
That I didn’t Google “how to center div” in the middle of a client demo like it was a spell.

So yes.  
ChatGPT called me an engineer.  
But I’ve always known the truth:

> I don’t solve problems.  
> I make them _believable_ enough to pass code review.

---

## Conclusion: Commit the Con

You think social engineering is tricking people into clicking fake emails?  
Please.  
That’s amateur hour.

Real social engineering is convincing your team you deserve write access.  
It’s renaming your Slack status to “debugging” when you’re just pacing around, questioning your life choices.  
It’s submitting a performance review that implies emotional resilience is a _deliverable._

So yes.  
Maybe I _am_ an engineer.  
But not because of what I build.

Because of what I _convince the stack_ to let me ship.
