The warehouse is quiet.

Too quiet.

A forklift hums past a row of shrink-wrapped bricks stamped in black ink:

**SARCASM — 98.7% PURE**

A man in a linen shirt and bad shoes slices one open with a box cutter.

Grey dust. Sharp smell.

He rubs a pinch between his fingers.

Snorts.

Pauses.

Then smiles.

“Yeah… that’s clean.”

The lieutenant leans in.

“How clean?”

The man exhales through his nose.

Not a laugh.

A _judgment_.

“This isn’t street sarcasm. This is executive-grade.”

He taps the brick.

“Lossy compressed.
No filler.
No empathy padding.
High MPPS.”

The lieutenant swallows.

“Boss… you think it’s too strong for retail?”

The man looks at him slowly.

“Retail?”

He gestures toward the pallets.

Each one stenciled:

**LSC — Lossy Sarcasm Codec**
**HANDLE WITH SOCIAL CONSEQUENCES**

“This isn’t for jokes.”

A beat.

“This is for boardrooms. Courtrooms. Family dinners.”

Another man walks in late.

Tracksuit. Gold chain. No sense of irony.

“Shipment from New Mexico just landed.”

The boss raises an eyebrow.

“Oh?”

“Yeah. New supplier.”

“What’s his name?”

The runner hesitates.

“…Ivan.”

Silence.

The boss closes the box cutter.

Slow.

Careful.

“Does it destabilize tone?”

The runner nods.

“Immediately.”

“Does it collapse polite conversation in under thirty seconds?”

“Yes, sir.”

The boss smiles for real now.

Not friendly.

“Good.”

He points at the pallets.

“Move the sarcasm through Denver.
Route the attitude through Austin.
Keep the pettiness offshore.”

The lieutenant looks nervous.

“And the really dangerous stuff?”

The boss turns.

Low voice.

“Kilos of…?”

“Contempt,” the runner says.

A pause.

The boss shakes his head.

“No. No. No.”

He taps one final crate in the corner.

Black label.

No branding.

Just a warning:

**MPPS: UNBOUNDED**

“That stays here.”

Everyone freezes.

“Why?”

The boss exhales.

Slow.

“Because once you release that much pettiness per syllable…”

He smiles.

“…you don’t control the conversation anymore.

You control the room.”
