## Software Studio Operating Model

Corporate Master Reference (Wiki Edition)

Applies To: Multi-tenant SaaS platforms built on Next.js, Prisma, Neon, Clerk, shadcn/ui

Audience: Executives, Directors, Team Leads, ICs

Status: Canonical

### 1\. Organizational Philosophy

This studio operates under functional specialization with enforced handoffs, not “everyone does everything and hopes for the best.”

Each team:

Owns a distinct domain

Produces explicit artifacts

Hands off verifiable outputs

Is accountable through measurable signals

The tech stack is not neutral. It dictates workflow, roles, and failure modes. This document reflects that reality.

### 2\. Team Overview (At a Glance)

| Team | Primary Mandate | Failure Mode if Weak |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operations | Business viability | Cash burn, chaos |
| Product | Direction & scope | Building nonsense |
| Design | Usability & clarity | Feature graveyard |
| Engineering | Execution | Vaporware |
| Marketing | Demand creation | Silent launch |

## 3\. Operations Team

### 3.1 Mission

Ensure the company exists, pays people, and survives contact with reality.

If Operations fails, no one ships anything because the lights are off.

### 3.2 Roles & Responsibilities

#### Core Roles

Operations Director

Finance & Compliance Manager

Vendor & Contract Administrator

People Ops / HR

#### Responsibilities

Company formation, governance, compliance

Budgeting, runway modeling, burn tracking

Vendor contracts (hosting, SaaS, tooling)

Payroll, benefits, contractor management

Risk management and legal coordination

### 3.3 Projects & Tasks

Financial forecasting models

Vendor cost optimization (Neon, Vercel, observability)

Billing reconciliation between Clerk and internal systems

Hiring pipelines and contractor onboarding

Policy documentation (security, access, data handling)

### 3.4 Outputs & Handoffs

Outputs

Approved budgets

Hiring approvals

Vendor SLAs

Financial reports

Handoffs

Budget constraints → Product

Hiring approvals → Engineering / Design

Compliance requirements → Engineering

### 3.5 Meetings & Coordination

Weekly leadership sync

Monthly financial review

Quarterly risk & compliance review

Operations never attends design critiques. That’s deliberate.

### 3.6 Resources

Accounting platforms

Legal counsel

HRIS

Vendor dashboards (billing, usage)

### 3.7 Scheduling & Tracking

Quarterly financial cycles

Monthly reporting cadence

Annual compliance calendar

### 3.8 Management & Reporting

Reports to: Executive Leadership

KPIs:

Runway length

Burn vs forecast

Vendor cost per user

Compliance incidents (should be zero)

## 4\. Product Team

### 4.1 Mission

Decide what should exist and why before Engineering wastes time implementing it.

Product does not design pixels or write code. Product defines truth.

### 4.2 Roles & Responsibilities

#### Core Roles

Head of Product

Product Manager

Product Analyst

#### Responsibilities

Product vision and roadmap

Feature prioritization

Requirement definition

Stakeholder alignment

Success metrics

### 4.3 Projects & Tasks

Product Requirement Documents (PRDs)

Feature scoping for RBAC, billing, tenant models

Plan tier definitions (Free, Pro, Enterprise)

User journey mapping

Backlog grooming

### 4.4 Outputs & Handoffs

Outputs

PRDs

Acceptance criteria

Roadmaps

Feature flags definitions

Handoffs

PRDs → Design

Scoped tickets → Engineering

Release notes → Marketing

If a feature is unclear, Product failed. Not Engineering.

### 4.5 Meetings & Coordination

Weekly roadmap review

Sprint planning with Engineering

Stakeholder demos

### 4.6 Resources

Product analytics tools

User research platforms

Roadmapping software

### 4.7 Scheduling & Tracking

Quarterly roadmaps

Sprint-level prioritization

Post-release metric review

### 4.8 Management & Reporting

Reports to: Executive Leadership

KPIs:

Feature adoption

Time-to-value

Churn by plan

Scope accuracy

## 8\. Cross-Team Coordination Model

Product defines what

Design defines how it feels

Engineering defines how it works

Marketing defines how it’s explained

Operations defines what’s allowed

No overlap. No ambiguity. No heroics.

### 9\. Conclusion

This structure exists to prevent:

Scope chaos

Feature thrash

Burnout

Delusion

It is boring on purpose.

That’s how real software studios ship real systems that survive real customers.
