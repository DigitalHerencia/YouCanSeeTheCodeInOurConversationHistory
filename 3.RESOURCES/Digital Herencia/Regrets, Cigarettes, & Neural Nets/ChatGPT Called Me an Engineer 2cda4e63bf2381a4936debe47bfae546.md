## But Let’s Be Honest…I Was Always a Social Engineer

---

At the end of 2025, my ChatGPT Year-in-Review flashed across the screen like some soft-focus graduation slide show.

> *“You sent 43,870 messages.”
“You wrote over 1.3 million words.”
“You asked 83 questions about how to fix CI/CD pipelines while clearly having an emotional breakdown.”*
> 

And then it hit me with the final punch:

> *“You're an engineer.”*
> 

And I sat there…barely hydrated, emotionally half-compiled, and all I could think was:

> No, I’m not. I’m a social engineer….with version control.
> 

![Most Likely to Refactor Reality.png](ChatGPT%20Called%20Me%20an%20Engineer/Most_Likely_to_Refactor_Reality.png)

## The Lie We Tell The Stack

---

I don’t debug. I *negotiate*.

I don’t code. I *manipulate abstractions until they comply*.

 

### You think I’m building systems?
I’m building *trust*, baby…Between modules. Between coworkers…Between reality and the README.

Every *working build* I’ve ever shipped was actually:

- a political compromise between three competing libraries
- a charm offensive against the CI/CD pipeline
- and a whispered lie to myself that *this is scalable*

## Dev Rel? Nah. Dev *Deception*

---

You think those pull requests were technical but they were *social theater*.
A dance of carefully worded comments and emoji reactions.
I merged that feature not because it worked, 
but because I made the backend *believe* it would.

![Still Life with Keyboard and Coffee Mug – ChatGPT, 2025.png](ChatGPT%20Called%20Me%20an%20Engineer/Still_Life_with_Keyboard_and_Coffee_Mug__ChatGPT_2025.png)

## My contribution wasn’t code.
It was vibes.

## The Terminal Is Just a Stage

---

You ever sit in a Zoom call and explain a bug you barely understand with enough confidence that someone else apologizes for it?

That’s not debugging.
That’s *psychological load balancing*.

I don’t run scripts.
I run *simulations* of competence until the problem goes away.

I’m not a developer.
I’m a con artist with a GitHub streak.

## My NPM Package Is Just a Cry for Help

---

I once published a utility that didn’t do anything but: 

 

`console.log("I'm trying my best.")`

 

Nobody starred it.
Nobody forked it.
I made a pull request to add support for *emotional dark mode.*

You tell me who the engineer is now.

## Engineering As Applied Storytelling

---

And the story I’ve been telling?
That I know what I’m doing.
That I’m not guessing every single day.
That I didn’t Google “how to center div” in the middle of a client demo like it was a prayer.

So yes.
ChatGPT called me an engineer.
But I’ve always known the truth:

> *I don’t solve problems.
 I make them believable enough to pass code review.*
> 

![year in poem.png](ChatGPT%20Called%20Me%20an%20Engineer/year_in_poem.png)

## Conclusion: Commit the Con

---

You think social engineering is tricking people into clicking fake emails?
Please.
That’s amateur hour.

Real social engineering is convincing your team you deserve write access.
It’s renaming your Slack status to “debugging” when you’re just pacing around, questioning your life choices.
It’s submitting a performance review that implies emotional resilience is a *deliverable*.

So yes.
Maybe I *am* an engineer.
But not because of what I build.

Because of what I *convince the stack* to let me ship.
