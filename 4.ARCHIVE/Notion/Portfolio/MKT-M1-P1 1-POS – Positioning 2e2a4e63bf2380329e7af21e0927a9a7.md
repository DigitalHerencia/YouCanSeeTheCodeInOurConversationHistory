# **T01: Positioning Statement**

---

## 1. Core Questions

### **1️⃣ What is it?**

For cannabis dispensary owners and retail operators in New Mexico who are underserved by generic analytics platforms and want an edge in a competitive, hyper-local market, **Competitive Advantage** is the *only* locally-built, culturally aware analytics platform that translates public sales data and shelf visibility into actionable, head-to-head insights for strategic stocking, pricing, and product curation.

### **2️⃣ Customer Benefits**

After using **Competitive Advantage**, retailers move from guesswork-driven operations to data-informed decision-making. Key benefits:

- Identify what to buy, and when
- Determine what products to remove and why
- Monitor competitive standing in real-time
- Capitalize on trending products early
- Anticipate event-driven sales surges
- Gain confidence and operational “swagger” through actionable insights

> It’s not just analytics—it’s strategic dominance. Data as dopamine. Winning made visible.
> 

### **3️⃣ Product Personality**

Unlike national platforms (Headset, BDSA) which are top-down and executive-focused, **Competitive Advantage** speaks operator-first:

- Direct and unapologetic
- Street-smart, regionally nuanced
- Designed for owners, managers, and analysts to act fast and with context

# **T03: Educational Content**

---

### 1️⃣ Problem Statement

New users fail **not due to lack of tools**, but due to:

- Fragmented mental models
- Inconsistent terminology
- Trial-and-error workflows under time pressure
- Documentation that assumes prior expertise

### 2️⃣ Educational Objectives

- **Normalize domain understanding:** Shared language, defined roles, system boundaries
- **Teach reasoning, not mechanics:** Explain *why* workflows exist, not just *how*
- **Accelerate competence:** Minimize support dependency, enable confident use

### 3️⃣ Scope

- Conceptual primers: domain overviews, system assumptions, misconceptions
- Operational explanations: mapping real workflows to product features, constraints
- Contextual learning: inline, progressive disclosure, role-specific guidance

### 4️⃣ Delivery Principles

- **Plainspoken:** No jargon without definition
- **Non-performative:** Authority through clarity, not hype
- **Composable:** Reusable in onboarding, documentation, in-app guidance

### 5️⃣ Success Metrics

- Users understand system expectations before action
- Reduced errors due to misunderstanding
- Support questions shift from “what” → “how to optimize”
- Product feels opinionated but fair

### 6️⃣ Non-Goals

- Replace formal documentation
- Teach advanced edge cases upfront
- Serve as marketing copy

### 1️⃣ Landscape Overview

**TAM:** ~9,500 US cannabis dispensaries

**Target Subset:** Licensed multi-location operators with >$2M revenue (~2,500)

# **T05: Product Language Feedback**

---

### 1️⃣ Observations Across T01–T04

| Area | Strength | Concern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positioning | Clear differentiation | Messaging high-level; may confuse non-technical users |
| Messaging Framework | Persona segmentation defined | Needs more actionable, sample messaging per persona |
| Educational Content | Strategy exists | Tone varies; technical-heavy for social sharing |
| Social Monitoring | Comprehensive plan | Messaging for external engagement not defined |

### 2️⃣ Key Issues

- **Terminology inconsistency** across dashboards, documentation, and marketing
- **Tone misalignment**: technical in T03, punchy in T01, descriptive in T04
- **Missing persona voice** in some content
- **Weak brand storytelling**: social insights not converted into external messaging
- **Content-to-market gap**: T03/T04 insights not fully feeding marketing campaigns

### 3️⃣ Recommendations

**3.1 Standardize Language**

- Centralized glossary and messaging matrix
- Ensure terminology aligns across dashboards, documentation, emails, social posts

**3.2 Persona-Centric Messaging**

- Map key product terms to Owners, Managers, Analysts, Admins
- Provide sample messaging for each persona
- Simplify technical/compliance-heavy language for social content

**3.3 Integrate Social Insights**

- Use T04 data to guide blog titles, social posts, webinars, emails
- Flag sentiment dips or pain points for content adjustment

**3.4 Audit Assets**

- Review all dashboards, emails, blogs, help content
- Identify top 10–15 terms for standardization
- Document tone-of-voice rules

**3.5 Feedback Loop**

- Marketing + Product review weekly insights
- Adjust content iteratively based on engagement trends
- A/B test dashboard microcopy and educational materials

### 4️⃣ Summary Perspective

**Strengths:** T01–T04 give strong structural foundations for positioning, education, and social monitoring

**Weaknesses:** Messaging consistency, tone, and persona alignment need work

**Opportunity:** Centralized product language framework + iterative feedback loop = clearer adoption path, stronger brand perception, and increased marketing impact pre-launch
