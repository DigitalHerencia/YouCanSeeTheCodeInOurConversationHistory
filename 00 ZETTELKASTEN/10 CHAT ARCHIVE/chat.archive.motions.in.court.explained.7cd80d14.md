A few common ones are:

"Motion to Suppress Evidence." This is the classic one from crime shows. The defense argues that evidence was obtained illegally, maybe through an unlawful search, seizure, interrogation, or identification procedure. Basically: "Your Honor, the cops got this evidence in a way the Constitution hates."

"Motion for Discovery." Discovery is the process where the prosecution turns over evidence. Police reports, witness statements, videos, lab results, and so on. Sometimes it's a request, sometimes a demand, sometimes a complaint that the other side isn't sharing the goodies.

"Motion to Dismiss." The legal equivalent of saying, "This case shouldn't even be here."

"Motion to Continue." Fancy lawyer language for "Can we move this hearing to another date because reasons?"

"Motion for Bond Reduction" or "Motion to Modify Conditions of Release." That's the "Can my client get out of jail cheaper?" motion.

"Motion in Limine." This one's got the Latin flavor you were craving. It basically asks the judge to prohibit certain evidence or arguments before trial starts.

"Motion to Compel." One side is refusing to hand something over or do something required, so the other side asks the judge to make them comply.

"Motion for Sanctions." Translation: "The other side is acting like a goblin, and we'd like consequences."

"Motion to Compel Discovery"
